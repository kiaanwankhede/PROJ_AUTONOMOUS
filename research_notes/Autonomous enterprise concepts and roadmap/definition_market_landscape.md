## 1. How do leading analysts and consultancies define the autonomous / agentic enterprise?

### Takeaway
No analyst or standards body has published a canonical definition of "autonomous enterprise." Gartner's term is **"autonomous business"**. McKinsey uses **"agentic organization"**, Microsoft **"Frontier Firm"**, Salesforce and BCG/MIT SMR **"agentic enterprise"**, HFS **"Services-as-Software"**, and IDC frames the shift as the **"agentic economy"** with **"AI-first"** processes. The exact phrase "autonomous enterprise" is mostly pushed by SAP (its flagship positioning since Sapphire 2026), by SAP partners (PwC), by automation vendors (Automation Anywhere, Celonis) and by IT-services and consulting firms (Genpact, EY, KPMG Belgium, HCLTech, Tech Mahindra). The definitions share a core: AI agents plus deterministic automation **sense, decide and act across end-to-end processes**, humans **set direction and policy and keep accountability**, and governance is built in. They differ on how much human oversight remains and on whether "autonomous" is a destination or a spectrum.

### Inferences
- The vocabulary splits along business models:
  - Analysts with no single platform to sell use neutral, outcome-oriented terms: Gartner "autonomous business", IDC "AI-first processes / agentic economy", Forrester "outcome economy", HFS "Services-as-Software".
  - Platform vendors and their SI partners use "autonomous enterprise" or "agentic enterprise", which map onto a product suite.
- Every definition, vendor or analyst, keeps humans in an accountable, direction-setting role ("people set the direction and AI executes", "humans holding final accountability", "human-on-the-loop"). **Nobody credible defines the autonomous enterprise as lights-out.** The real disagreement is how far toward L4/L5 the typical process should go, and how fast.
- The 2026 analyst consensus has moved from "agents everywhere" (2024–25) to **governed, selective autonomy**:
  - Forrester: fewer than 10% will run cross-app autonomy, and 20% of agentic workflows will revert to deterministic processes.
  - IDC: kill switches and continuous verification become insurance and audit issues.
  - Everest: SAP's vision is "not a proof".
  - Gartner: proportional governance by autonomy level (see Q5).

## 2. Lineage of the term, and which vendors explicitly use "autonomous enterprise" (and what products back the claim) as of late 2026

### Takeaway
The idea runs from IBM's **autonomic computing** (2001: self-managing IT with self-configure, self-heal, self-optimize and self-protect, plus a 5-level maturity model in 2002). It continues through the **"self-driving" metaphor** (Aera's "Self-Driving Enterprise", c. 2017–18), Gartner's **hyperautomation** (top trend for 2020, announced Oct 2019) and **autonomous business / machine customers** (2023 onward), and **agentic AI** as Gartner's #1 trend for 2025 (Oct 2024). The 2025 organizational labels followed: Microsoft's **Frontier Firm** (Apr 2025), McKinsey's **agentic organization** (Sep 2025) and Salesforce's **Agentic Enterprise / Agentforce 360** (Oct 2025). In 2026, **SAP made "Autonomous Enterprise" its flagship positioning** (Sapphire, May 2026). ServiceNow branded "Agentic Business" and an "Autonomous Workforce", and the hyperscalers recast their platforms as agent control planes. In late 2026 the vendors that explicitly use the phrase "autonomous enterprise" are SAP, Automation Anywhere, Salesforce (content marketing), Celonis, and SIs and consultancies (PwC, Genpact, EY, KPMG BE, HCLTech, Tech Mahindra).

### Cited Findings

#### Timeline (lineage)
- **2001, IBM autonomic computing.** Paul Horn presented the theme in March 2001 at the US National Academy of Engineering. In October 2001 IBM released a 38-page "manifesto" arguing that the complexity of computing systems had begun to outpace human administrators' ability to cope. — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/pc-hardware/making-a-computer-take-care-of-itself/); [Ganek & Corbi, IBM Systems Journal (PDF)](https://people.ece.ubc.ca/~matei/EECE571.09/Papers/ganek.pdf)
- IBM's four self-* properties are self-configuration, self-healing, self-optimization and self-protection. It added eight conditions: know itself, reconfigure, optimize, repair, protect, adapt and communicate with neighbors, open standards, and anticipate demand while staying transparent. The Monitor-Analyze-Plan-Execute (MAPE) control loop uses sensors, effectors, knowledge and policies. IBM announced a **five-level "autonomic deployment model"** on 21 Oct 2002, from Level 1 (basic, manually managed) to Level 5 (fully autonomic, self-managing). — [Wikipedia: Autonomic computing (fetched)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomic_computing); [Stanford summary](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/eroberts/courses/soco/projects/2003-04/fault-tolerant-computing/how-autonomic.html)

- The lineage shows a constant pattern: a **closed control loop** (sense, analyze, decide, act) first applied to infrastructure (autonomic computing 2001, autonomous networks), then to processes (hyperautomation 2019), and now to cross-functional business decisions through LLM agents (2024–26). "Agentic enterprise" is associated with CRM and workflow platforms (Salesforce, ServiceNow).

## 3. What autonomy maturity models / levels exist?

### Takeaway
Most enterprise and agent autonomy scales copy the **SAE J3016 L0–L5 driving-levels** template, usually via **TM Forum's Autonomous Networks L0–L5**, which explicitly divides execution, awareness, analysis, decision and intent between people and systems. Three families exist:
1. **Who holds which function** (TM Forum; Capgemini L0–L5).
2. **What role the human plays** (Feng et al. operator → observer; Microsoft's phases and modes).
3. **What the agent technically controls** (Hugging Face levels; Bain and Salesforce capability levels; AWS agency scopes).

The most useful insight for engineering is AWS's and Feng et al.'s point that **autonomy is a design choice separate from capability or agency**. Gartner (May 2026) says governance should be proportional to autonomy level.

### Cited Findings

#### Industry-body / standards analogues
- **TM Forum Autonomous Networks** [Industry body; level table as reproduced by Cisco, updated 7 Mar 2025, fetched].
  - Level names: **L0 Manual Operation & Maintenance; L1 Assisted O&M; L2 Partial Autonomous Networks; L3 Conditional Autonomous Networks; L4 High Autonomous Networks; L5 Full Autonomous Networks.**
  - Cisco's descriptions: L1 "basic automation with manual intervention"; L2 "some automated processes… significant manual oversight"; L3 AI agents manage operations "with minimal manual intervention inside an autonomous domain"; L4 "proactive automation… across multiple domains… self-healing"; L5 "comprehensive end-to-end automation across all network domains… seamless integration of business processes".

    | Function | L0 | L1 | L2 | L3 | L4 | L5 |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | Execution | P | P/S | S | S | S | S |
    | Awareness | P | P/S | P/S | S | S | S |
    | Analysis | P | P | P/S | P/S | S | S |
    | Decision | P | P | P | P/S | S | S |
    | Intent/Experience | P | P | P | P | P/S | S |

#### Historical
- **IBM autonomic deployment model** (21 Oct 2002): five levels, from Level 1 "basic" (manual management) through increasingly automated Levels 2–4 to Level 5 "autonomic" (self-managing). — [Wikipedia: Autonomic computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomic_computing)

#### Academic
- **Feng, McDonald and Zhang (University of Washington), "Levels of Autonomy for AI Agents"** (arXiv 2506.12469, Jun 2025, rev. Jul 2025; Knight First Amendment Institute). Levels are defined by the **user's role**:
  - **L1 Operator**: user directs and decides, agent acts.
  - **L2 Collaborator**: they plan, delegate and execute together.
  - **L3 Consultant**: agent leads, asks for user expertise and preferences.
  - **L4 Approver**: user is involved only for risky or pre-specified cases.
  - **L5 Observer**: agent is fully autonomous, user monitors.
  - Autonomy is treated as a **design decision separate from capability**, and the paper proposes "AI autonomy certificates" for single- and multi-agent systems.
  - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12469); [Knight Institute](https://knightcolumbia.org/content/levels-of-autonomy-for-ai-agents-1)
- **Mitchell, Ghosh, Luccioni and Pistilli (Hugging Face), "Fully Autonomous AI Agents Should Not be Developed"** (arXiv 2502.02649, Feb 2025, rev. Oct 2025, fetched).
  - Defines AI agents as "computer software systems capable of creating context-specific plans in non-deterministic environments".
  - Table 1 levels: ☆☆☆☆ **Simple processor** (human in control; `print_llm_output`); ★☆☆☆ **Router** (model decides basic flow; `if llm_decision()`); ★★☆☆ **Tool caller** (model picks what function and how; `run_function(llm_chosen_tool, llm_chosen_args)`); ★★★☆ **Multi-step agent** (model controls iteration; `while should_continue()`); ★★★★ **Fully autonomous agent** (system creates and executes new code).
  - Thesis: "We argue that fully autonomous AI agents should not be developed."
  - [arXiv HTML (fetched)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02649v3)
- Follow-on use in 2026: the TrustX Agent Risk Classification framework (arXiv 2607.09586, Jul 2026) adopts Feng et al.'s levels and demands more rigorous governance controls at higher levels. The 2025 AI Agent Index (arXiv 2602.17753) also uses a levels framing. — [TrustX ARC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09586); [2025 AI Agent Index](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17753)

#### Analyst / consultancy models
- **Capgemini Research Institute, levels of autonomy in AI agents** (Jul 2025, verbatim from report):

  | Level | Capabilities | Human involvement |
  |---|---|---|
  | **0: No agent involvement** | No additional capabilities | Fully handled by humans |
  | **1: AI-assisted (automation first)** | "Deterministic systems that always produce identical outputs, given identical inputs" | High human involvement; AI assists with predefined workflows |
  | **2: AI-augmented decision-making** | Agents support decisions and give recommendations | Humans retain control |
  | **3: AI-integrated (process-centric AI)** | "Semi-autonomous AI agents handle complex tasks" | "Human delegates authority in specific areas… but is actively involved in management" |

  | **5: Fully autonomous (self-evolving systems)** | "Fully autonomous execution of processes towards a specified goal within the defined scope" | Humans only for "goal setting and monitoring, compliance and strategic governance" |

  - **L0** fixed rules and repetitive tasks (chatbots and copilots that don't recommend actions).
  - **L1** information-retrieval agents that recommend actions.
  - **L2** simple orchestration in a single domain (low-complexity tasks, siloed data).
  - **L3** complex orchestration across multiple domains with harmonized data.
  - **L4** multi-agent orchestration ("any-to-any-agent operability across disparate stacks with agent supervision"), which Salesforce says no one has reached yet.
  - [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/agentic-maturity-model/); [Salesforce blog](https://www.salesforce.com/uk/blog/agentic-maturity/)
- **AWS Agentic AI Security Scoping Matrix** (2025):
  - **Scope 1 No Agency**: human-initiated, read-only, fixed paths.
  - **Scope 2 Prescribed Agency**: human approval required for all actions.
  - **Scope 3 Supervised Agency**: autonomous execution after a human starts the task, with optional guidance.
  - **Scope 4 Full Agency**: self-initiating, continuous operation with minimal oversight.
  - AWS distinguishes **agency** ("the scope of actions an AI system is permitted and enabled to take") from **autonomy** (how independently it acts). An agent can have high agency and low autonomy.
  - The Cloud Security Alliance (16 Dec 2025) critiques the "No Agency" label and proposes a multi-dimensional alternative.
  - [AWS scoping matrix](https://aws.amazon.com/ai/security/agentic-ai-scoping-matrix/); [AWS Security Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/the-agentic-ai-security-scoping-matrix-a-framework-for-securing-autonomous-ai-systems); [CSA](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2025/12/16/enhancing-the-agentic-ai-security-scoping-matrix-a-multi-dimensional-approach)
- **Automation Anywhere, five-stage evolution** (fetched; vendor):
  1. Human-led AI assistance.
  2. AI-augmented decisions.
  3. Balanced human-AI collaboration (humans make final decisions).
  4. AI-led with human oversight of critical decisions and exceptions.
  5. "Autonomous enterprise with fully autonomous operations", where humans focus on strategy and AI governance.
  - [Automation Anywhere](https://www.automationanywhere.com/rpa/autonomous-enterprise)

### Inferences
- **Approximate crosswalk** (my mapping; level boundaries do not align exactly across frameworks):

  | Generic level | TM Forum AN | Capgemini | Feng et al. (user role) | AWS scope | Salesforce | Bain |

  | 0: Manual / no agent | L0 | L0 | n/a | n/a | L0 (rules) | n/a |

- The TM Forum P/S matrix is the most operational template for an enterprise. It can be applied per process (for example order-to-cash or incident-to-resolution) by recording which of sense, analyze, decide, act and set intent are done by systems. An "autonomous enterprise" is then a **portfolio-level distribution** of process autonomy levels, not a single binary label. Capgemini's survey (15% → 25% of processes at L3 or higher) and IDC's "majority of processes AI-first by 2029" are both distribution-style measures, which supports this approach.
- L5 is disfavored: Hugging Face argues fully autonomous agents should not be built, Capgemini finds only 2–4% of processes expected at L5, and Forrester says cross-app autonomy will reach fewer than 10% of enterprises. A defensible enterprise target is therefore **L3–L4 per process, with an explicit human intent and accountability layer**.

## 6. What is the most defensible, vendor-neutral working definition of an "autonomous enterprise" for a Gen AI engineering team?

### Takeaway
Combine the shared elements of the analyst definitions (Gartner autonomous business, McKinsey agentic organization, IDC AI-first processes) with the operational rigor of TM Forum's level matrix and the agency-versus-autonomy distinction from AWS and Feng et al. Proposed definition: **"An autonomous enterprise is an organization in which a material, measured share of its end-to-end business processes run as governed closed loops (sensing events, deciding, and acting across systems of record through AI agents and deterministic automation) under explicitly delegated, revocable authority, while humans set intent and policy, handle escalations, and retain accountability."** Autonomy is declared and measured **per process on an L0–L5 scale**, not claimed for the company as a whole.

### Cited Findings (building blocks)
- **Decide and act, not just inform; both people and machine autonomy grow.** Gartner: autonomous business uses "self-improving, adaptable technology to make decisions, take action and create new types of value by increasing both people autonomy and machine autonomy". — [Gartner, 2026-05-18](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-18-gartner-says-chief-supply-chain-officers-must-shift-to-an-autonomous-business-mindset)
- **Humans and agents at scale; embedded governance; human accountability.** McKinsey: "unites humans and AI agents… at scale at near-zero marginal cost", with governance "real time, data driven, and embedded—with humans holding final accountability". — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/the-agentic-organization-contours-of-the-next-paradigm-for-the-ai-era)
- **Process-level, portfolio measure.** IDC: "a majority of business processes AI-first", 13% (2026) → 60% (2029). Capgemini: share of processes at L3 or higher, 15% → 25% by 2028. — [IDC](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/idc-futurescape-2027-predicts-60-170700479.html); [Capgemini](https://www.capgemini.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Final-Web-Version-Report-AI-Agents.pdf)
- **Closed loop with intent at the top.** TM Forum: the people-versus-systems split across execution, awareness, analysis, decision and intent defines the level, and intent stays with people until L4. — [Cisco/TM Forum table](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/solutions/collateral/networking/auton-ntwk-sp-wp.html)
- **Autonomy is a design parameter separate from capability and agency.** — [Feng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12469); [AWS Security Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/the-agentic-ai-security-scoping-matrix-a-framework-for-securing-autonomous-ai-systems)
- **Governance proportional to autonomy level; verification and kill switches as hard requirements.** — [Gartner, 2026-05-26](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-26-gartner-says-applying-uniform-governance-across-ai-agents-will-lead-to-enterprise-ai-agent-failure); [IDC FutureScape 2027](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/futurescape-2027-three-trust-decisions-are-coming/)
- **Agents plus deterministic automation, not agents alone.** Automation Anywhere's definition orchestrates "AI agents alongside deterministic automation and human oversight". Forrester predicts 20% of agentic workflows will revert to deterministic processes. — [Automation Anywhere](https://www.automationanywhere.com/rpa/autonomous-enterprise); [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/predictions-2027-automation-initiatives-become-more-considered/)
- **Cross-functional linkage, not task automation.** EY: the autonomous enterprise differs from simple task automation because it links intelligence across functions. A vendor blog adds: "You can have agentic AI without an autonomous enterprise — a single clever agent bolted onto one workflow". — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_in/insights/ai/the-next-frontier-of-ai-building-autonomous-enterprises); [Ampcome (vendor blog)](https://www.ampcome.com/post/best-autonomous-enterprise-ai-platforms-compared)
- **Full autonomy is neither expected nor advisable for most processes.** Hugging Face position paper; Capgemini: L5 about 2–4% of processes; Forrester: under 10% cross-app autonomy. — [Mitchell et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02649v3); [Capgemini](https://www.capgemini.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Final-Web-Version-Report-AI-Agents.pdf); [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/predictions-2027-the-outcome-economy-hits-enterprise-software-and-reality-hits-back/)

### Inferences
- **Working definition (recommended for adoption):** *An autonomous enterprise is an organization in which a material, measured share of its end-to-end business processes run as governed closed loops (sense → analyze → decide → act → learn) executed by AI agents and deterministic automation across its systems of record. Each process runs under an explicit, revocable delegation of authority (scope, risk thresholds, spend limits, escalation rules, kill switch). Humans set intent and policy, adjudicate exceptions, and remain accountable for outcomes.*
- **Terminology the team should keep distinct:**
  - **Agentic AI** is the *capability*: goal-directed systems that plan and use tools.
  - **Agentic enterprise or organization** is the *operating model* built around human-agent teams.
  - **Autonomous enterprise** is the *degree of delegated decision and action authority actually achieved*, measured per process.
  - **Agency** is what an agent *can* do (permissions and tools); **autonomy** is how much it does *without human approval*.
- **Measurement proposal** (synthesized from TM Forum, Capgemini and IDC):
  - Score each material process L0–L5 using a sense/analyze/decide/act/intent P-versus-S matrix.
  - Report the distribution, for example the percentage of processes and of transaction volume at L3 or higher.
  - Track for each process: straight-through-processing rate, escalation rate, human-override and reversal rate, incident and rollback count, cost per outcome, decision latency, and the human-agent ratio (Microsoft's metric).
  - "Autonomous enterprise" is then a threshold the team sets, for example a majority of material process volume at L3 or higher with L4 in selected domains. It should not be a slogan. No analyst sets a numeric threshold; this one is borrowed from IDC's "majority of processes AI-first" framing.
- **Disqualifiers (anti-agent-washing tests):**
  - Assistants that only retrieve or suggest (L1).
  - Single-task bots with no cross-system action.
  - Autonomy without identity, audit trail, runtime verification and revocation.
  - "Autonomy" that cannot be dialed down per process.
  - These follow Gartner's agent-washing definition and IDC's and Gartner's 2026 governance predictions.
- **Calibration for 2026–2028 planning:** independent evidence puts most enterprises at L1–L2, with pockets of L3 (Gartner 17% deployed; Deloitte 11% in production; Capgemini 15% of processes at L3 or higher in 12 months; telecom averages about L2.5–2.8 after roughly seven years of TM Forum work). A realistic engineering roadmap targets **L3 in bounded, high-volume, exception-heavy processes**, which is also Everest's recommendation for SAP customers. L4 comes only where verification, rollback and liability controls are in place.

### Gaps
- No standards body (ISO/IEC, NIST, IEEE) definition of "autonomous enterprise" or of enterprise-level autonomy levels was found. A search for ISO/IEC 22989 or 42001 agent-autonomy terminology was not performed because the search budget ran out.
- No empirical benchmark links process-autonomy levels to financial outcomes. The available evidence (BCG 2.4x top-line growth for "future-built" firms; Capgemini's $382M vs $76M) is correlational and comes from consultancy surveys.
